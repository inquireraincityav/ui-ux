<CsSection label="Context & Problem">
  <CsStatement>
    <span class="accent">Adventure Awaits</span> is a camping discovery website for BC families who want to explore the outdoors but don't know where to start.
  </CsStatement>

  First-time campers face a common set of frustrations: they don't know how or where to start, it's hard to find what activities are offered at each campsite, and booking information is scattered across multiple sources. The site needed to make campsite discovery feel approachable - not overwhelming.

  The goal was a single destination where families could quickly overview a campsite, view prices and booking links, and plan activities - all in a visual language that felt fun, friendly, and family-oriented.
</CsSection>

<CsSection label="Visual Language">
  <CsStatement>
    The moodboard established six keywords that drove every visual decision: fun, friendly, bright, informative, simple, and family
  </CsStatement>

  A bright colour scheme creates a playful and energetic feeling - welcoming and approachable, signalling that families will have a fun time at the campsites. Mountain and nature flat shapes introduce informality without distracting from the content, while rectangular coloured info cards summarize details in a scannable format.

  Flat icons help users take in information more easily than large blocks of text, while conveying a playful tone. Overlaying text on flat shapes extends the nature theme into the background and creates visual hierarchy. **Poppins**, a simple sans-serif font, balances out the visual noise from the colours and shapes so the interface doesn't overwhelm.

<CsSection label="Design Principles">
  <CsStatement>
    Four principles kept the design family-friendly without sacrificing usability
  </CsStatement>

  Every decision was checked against whether Blake - a first-time camper with his family - could complete the task on the first try.

  <CsPrincipleGrid>
    <CsPrinciple title="Playful Clarity">
      Bright colours and flat shapes create energy, but simple sans-serif type and structured layouts keep the information readable and scannable.
    </CsPrinciple>
    <CsPrinciple title="Scannable Content">
      Rectangular info cards, flat icons, and short copy let busy families absorb key details at a glance without reading paragraphs.
    </CsPrinciple>
    <CsPrinciple title="Clear Actions">
      Contrasting colours on call-to-action buttons make the next step obvious - find a campsite, view details, or book now.
    </CsPrinciple>
    <CsPrinciple title="Mobile First">
      Responsive layouts, a hamburger menu with highlighted search, and touch-friendly targets ensure the experience works where families actually browse - on their phones.
    </CsPrinciple>
  </CsPrincipleGrid>
</CsSection>

<CsSection label="Next Steps">
  <CsStatement>
    Future iterations would deepen personalisation and build community around the camping experience
  </CsStatement>

  An interactive campsite finder with personalised recommendations and filters for beginner-friendly sites, activities, and amenities would reduce decision fatigue. A community section for reviews, photos, and Q&A forums would help first-time campers learn from others, while gamification features like badges and rewards could boost engagement.

  Accessibility enhancements - voice search and a high-contrast mode - would improve usability for all users. Integrating real-time campsite availability and weather updates would make the booking process feel more trustworthy and reduce the back-and-forth of checking external sources.
</CsSection>

<CsSection label="Reflections">
  <CsStatement>
    This project taught me how to balance visual energy with functional clarity
  </CsStatement>

  **Bright colours work when the layout does the heavy lifting.** The playful palette was effective in making the experience engaging and family-friendly, but maintaining clarity and readability was crucial. Simple sans-serif fonts and structured layouts balanced the visual noise.

  **First-time users need clear next steps, not more options.** Users like Blake struggle with decision-making, so clear call-to-action buttons significantly improved navigation. Users prefer quick, summarised information, making the rectangular info cards an effective way to communicate details.

  **Mobile isn't a secondary concern - it's the primary one.** Since many users access the site on mobile, ensuring intuitive navigation, responsive design, and clear search functions was key. The hamburger menu and search bar highlight made browsing easier.

  **Iterative feedback catches what assumptions miss.** Usability tests showed that users valued visual differentiation for sections but needed clearer actionable steps for booking. Mentor guidance reinforced the importance of contrasting colours for clarity while ensuring the UI remains cohesive.
</CsSection>
