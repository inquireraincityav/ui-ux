## What SeniorCircle is

SeniorCircle is a social-events app built specifically for adults 75 and older
 - a group most consumer apps are designed against, not for. It helps them
discover local events tailored to their interests, book seats, and see which
events their friends are already going to.

The core insight: general-purpose event apps optimize for choice and density,
which is exactly what someone with aging vision, reduced motor precision, and
lower digital confidence finds overwhelming. **A senior-first app is not a
seniors-only app - it's the same job, done at a different tempo.**

Older users don't need a stripped-down toy. They need larger type, simpler
navigation, and features that address the real reason they downloaded the
app in the first place: they're lonely.

## Who it's for

We built the design around one specific person: **Arsh Singh**, a 70-year-old
retired schoolteacher who recently moved to Canada to live with his son.
He spends most of his days alone while his son and daughter-in-law are at
work. His technology proficiency is intermediate - comfortable with a phone
but not fluent - and he wears corrective lenses.

- **Interests:** reading, praying, walking
- **Goals:** engage in social activities with people who share his interests;
  meet new people and build a community in a new country
- **Pain point:** can't find where to look. Community centers exist but
  aren't discoverable online. Facebook events are dominated by younger crowds.
  The apps he's tried assume he already knows the local scene.

Arsh became our design constant. Every decision - type size, gesture,
information order - was checked against whether *he* could complete the task
on the first try.

## Design process

We started with a full information architecture pass - mapping every screen
the app would need and how a user would move between them. Working this out
on paper before opening Figma kept us honest about scope for a three-week
project.

## Visual language

The style guide is where accessibility became a design constraint, not a
compliance checklist. We chose **Bodoni 72 Oldstyle** for the app name - a
familiar warm serif for a demographic that grew up reading in serif - paired
with **Verdana** as the workhorse body face, chosen for its exceptionally
wide apertures and unambiguous letterforms at large sizes. A muted red
functions as the primary action color, cream as the backdrop for reduced
eye strain, and blue as the secondary for state and confirmation.

## What I'd do next

- **Usability testing with real users** across a range of ages, tech
  fluency, and visual acuity. The design was built for Arsh, but only real
  seniors can tell us where it still assumes too much.
- **Microinteractions** - subtle animation on state changes to make the
  interface feel responsive without being distracting. Reduced-motion mode
  as a first-class setting, not an afterthought.
- **A pass against WCAG 2.2** on the shipped build - contrast, focus states,
  screen reader labels, motor accommodations.
- **Performance and offline states** with an engineering partner - many of
  our target users have inconsistent connectivity and slower devices.
- **Feature expansion** based on real usage: personalized recommendations,
  onboarding that adapts to the user's confidence level, richer messaging.

## What I learned

- **Designing for a demographic you're not part of takes humility.** Every
  assumption I made early - that seniors want "simple," that they don't want
  animation, that they need a tutorial - was wrong in some way. Personas
  and interviews caught the drift before it shipped.
- **Aesthetics and function aren't in tension when the aesthetics *are* the
  function.** The warm serif and cream palette look editorial, but their
  primary job was legibility and warmth. That's not decoration.
- **Prototyping early saves the expensive revisions.** ProtoPie let us put
  the signup flow in front of two seniors before we had touched hi-fi.
  Their confusion at step 3 rewrote the flow before it was drawn.
- **Collaboration keeps the design honest.** Working with Jaival and Harsh
  meant every choice had to be defended out loud. Half of my best decisions
  were their pushback.

This was a formative project - the first time I built for a group I'd never
been part of. The learnings ripple through everything I've designed since.
