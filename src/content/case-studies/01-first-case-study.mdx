<CsSection label="Context & Problem">
  <CsStatement>
    <span class="accent">SeniorCircle</span> is a social discovery app for adults 75 and older - a group most consumer apps are designed against, not for.
  </CsStatement>

  General-purpose event apps optimize for choice and density, which is exactly what someone with aging vision, reduced motor precision, and lower digital confidence finds overwhelming. **A senior-first app is not a seniors-only app - it's the same job, done at a different tempo.**

  SeniorCircle helps seniors discover local events tailored to their interests, book seats, and see which events their friends are already going to. Older users don't need a stripped-down toy. They need larger type, simpler navigation, and features that address the real reason they downloaded the app in the first place: they're lonely.
</CsSection>

<CsSection label="Target User">
  <CsStatement>
    We built the design around one specific person: Arsh Singh, a 70-year-old retired schoolteacher who recently moved to Canada
  </CsStatement>

  He spends most of his days alone while his son and daughter-in-law are at work. His technology proficiency is intermediate - comfortable with a phone but not fluent - and he wears corrective lenses. Arsh became our design constant. Every decision - type size, gesture, information order - was checked against whether he could complete the task on the first try.

<CsSection label="Design Process">
  <CsStatement>
    We started with a full information architecture pass - mapping every screen the app would need and how a user would move between them
  </CsStatement>

  Working this out on paper before opening Figma kept us honest about scope for a three-week project. Login and profile setup collapse into a five-step signup that ends on the home screen. The four main tabs are Home, Create an Event, Bookings, and Profile.

<CsSection label="Visual Language">
  <CsStatement>
    The style guide is where accessibility became a design constraint, not a compliance checklist
  </CsStatement>

  We chose **Bodoni 72 Oldstyle** for the app name - a familiar warm serif for a demographic that grew up reading in serif - paired with **Verdana** as the workhorse body face, chosen for its exceptionally wide apertures and unambiguous letterforms at large sizes. A muted red functions as the primary action color, cream as the backdrop for reduced eye strain, and blue as the secondary for state and confirmation.

<CsSection label="Design Principles">
  <CsStatement>
    Through our research and iteration, we aligned around four guiding principles that shaped every design decision
  </CsStatement>

  These principles helped balance accessibility with engagement - **ensuring that the SeniorCircle experience felt both welcoming and capable.**

  <CsPrincipleGrid>
    <CsPrinciple title="Legibility">
      18pt minimum body type, high-contrast color pairings, and generous whitespace so every screen reads comfortably with corrective lenses.
    </CsPrinciple>
    <CsPrinciple title="Simplicity">
      One task per screen, plain-language copy, and a flat navigation structure that never makes the user guess where they are.
    </CsPrinciple>
    <CsPrinciple title="Community">
      Events friends are going to surface first, then events nearby, then personalized recommendations - social proof drives engagement.
    </CsPrinciple>
    <CsPrinciple title="Trust">
      Generous tap targets, forgiving input fields, and confirmation steps that reduce anxiety around accidental actions.
    </CsPrinciple>
  </CsPrincipleGrid>
</CsSection>

<CsSection label="Reflections">
  <CsStatement>
    This was a formative project - the first time I built for a group I'd never been part of
  </CsStatement>

  **Designing for a demographic you're not part of takes humility.** Every assumption I made early - that seniors want "simple," that they don't want animation, that they need a tutorial - was wrong in some way. Personas and interviews caught the drift before it shipped.

  **Aesthetics and function aren't in tension when the aesthetics are the function.** The warm serif and cream palette look editorial, but their primary job was legibility and warmth. That's not decoration.

  **Prototyping early saves the expensive revisions.** ProtoPie let us put the signup flow in front of two seniors before we had touched hi-fi. Their confusion at step 3 rewrote the flow before it was drawn.

  **Collaboration keeps the design honest.** Working with Jaival and Harsh meant every choice had to be defended out loud. Half of my best decisions were their pushback. The learnings ripple through everything I've designed since.
</CsSection>
