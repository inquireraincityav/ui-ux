## What Clove By Quantum is

CLOVE is a collection of 11 premium townhomes in the heart of Cloverdale,
Surrey, BC - built by Quantum Properties and sold through the JR Group.
The project needed a website that could do what a sales centre can't:
reach buyers before they drive across town.

The site had to serve three jobs at once: **showcase the development**
with renders and floor plans, **sell the neighbourhood** to people who
may never have heard of Cloverdale, and **capture leads** for the sales
team. All of it within 30 days, working solo.

## The brief

Quantum Properties had no online presence for CLOVE. Prospective buyers
had no way to browse floor plans, understand the neighbourhood, or
register interest without physically visiting the sales centre. In a
market where most buyers start their search online, that gap was costing
them reach.

They needed a professional, visually rich website that could stand
alongside established developers - one that built awareness, generated
leads, and answered the questions buyers have before they pick up the
phone.

## Discovery and structure

The process started with the client's goals: who are the buyers, what
do they need to see, and in what order? Two priorities surfaced quickly.
First, the hero moment had to feel premium - a video walkthrough, not a
static image. Second, the site needed to sell *Cloverdale itself*, not
just the homes. Many buyers in the Lower Mainland don't know the area,
so a dedicated location section was essential.

I mapped the site into five sections: **Home** (hero video, about
snippet, location teaser, contact), **Features** (interior details and
renders), **Location** (neighbourhood amenities and map), **Gallery**
(photography), and a persistent **registration CTA** across all pages.

## Iterative design

The build was iterative - prototyping in Figma, building in Wix, and
incorporating client feedback at every stage. Key revisions shaped the
final product:

**Homepage.** The entrance video was swapped for a version starting at
a specific timestamp that better represented the development. The "About
Clove" heading was enlarged for visual hierarchy. A new Location teaser
section was added with a "Read More" CTA leading to the dedicated
Location tab - content that had been buried was now discoverable from
the front page.

**About page.** The original embedded video was replaced with a
high-resolution townhome render - a static focal point that loaded
faster and communicated more. The Quantum Properties logo was added to
reinforce the developer's reputation. Content was streamlined to focus
on one thing: who Quantum is and why that matters.

**New tabs.** The Features tab consolidated interior details and renders
that had been scattered across the About page. The Location tab became
its own experience - a three-column amenities grid covering recreation,
education, shopping, and healthcare, backed by a photo gallery of the
neighbourhood.

## Selling the neighbourhood

One of the more interesting design problems was making Cloverdale itself
feel like a feature of the product. Many prospective buyers knew Surrey
but not this specific corner of it. The Location tab needed to answer
"why here?" before the buyer ever asked.

The solution was a dedicated gallery of neighbourhood landmarks - the
skate park, local shops, community centres - presented in a carousel
that felt editorial, not like a stock-photo dump. Combined with the
amenities grid above it, the page builds a picture of daily life in the
area, not just proximity data.

## Lead capture and contact

The bottom of every page closes with the same structure: the sales
advisor's contact card, a direct link to the sales centre, and a
persistent registration CTA. The goal was zero dead ends - no matter
where a visitor finished reading, the next step was always visible.

## What I learned

- **Selling a neighbourhood is a design problem.** The homes were the
  product, but the location was the pitch. Structuring the site to sell
  Cloverdale first and the townhomes second changed the entire
  information architecture - and it was the right call.
- **Iterative client feedback saves time, not costs it.** Every round of
  revisions caught something that would have been harder to fix after
  launch. The video timestamp change, the About page simplification, the
  new tab structure - all came from feedback loops, not the first draft.
- **Performance is part of the design.** The client's earlier gallery
  attempts had loading issues. Solving that on the Wix build wasn't a
  dev task bolted on at the end - it was a design constraint from day one
  that shaped how the gallery was structured.
- **Solo projects teach you where your defaults are.** Without a team to
  challenge decisions, I had to be my own critic. The discipline of
  justifying each choice - even privately - caught assumptions I would
  have shipped otherwise.
